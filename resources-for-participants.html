Resources for Participants

Understanding your rights, staying safe online, and knowing why your participation matters.

1. Your rights as a research participant

Taking part in research is voluntary. Before participating, you should be informed about:

  • The purpose of the study and what participation involves.
  • The approximate time required.
  • Any possible discomfort, inconvenience or risks.
  • Whether your information will be collected anonymously or confidentially.
  • How your information will be stored and used.
  • Whether you can withdraw and how to do so.
  • Whom to contact if you have questions or concerns.

You have the right to refuse participation. You should not feel pressured to participate, and refusal should not affect your access to healthcare, education or other services.

Further reading: ICMR National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research Involving Human Participants (2017). These guidelines describe voluntary participation, informed consent, privacy, confidentiality and protection of participants' rights.

2. Protect your personal information during online surveys

Important safety advice

Never share OTPs, passwords, PINs or banking credentials with a researcher or through a research questionnaire. Before completing an online questionnaire, remember that you are sharing information through the internet.

Avoid sharing unnecessary identifying or financial information, including:

  • Full name, residential address or exact location.
  • Aadhaar number or Aadhaar card photographs.
  • PAN number or PAN card photographs.
  • Bank account numbers, passbook photographs or financial details.
  • ATM/debit/credit card details.
  • OTPs, passwords, PINs or UPI PINs.
  • Personal mobile number or email address, unless genuinely required and adequately explained.
  • Photographs, identity documents or other information that could reveal your identity.

3. How to check whether a research study is genuine

Before participating, consider the following:

  1. Is the principal investigator's name and institutional affiliation provided?
  2. Is the purpose of the study clearly explained?
  3. Is Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) approval mentioned, with an approval reference?
  4. Is there a participant information sheet and consent statement?
  5. Are contact details provided for questions or concerns?
  6. Does the questionnaire ask for information that seems unrelated or unnecessarily sensitive?

If you have doubts, independently contact the institution or its ethics committee using contact information obtained from the institution's official website. Do not rely solely on the contact details or claims provided in an unfamiliar survey link.

4. Important disclaimer

GujPsychSurvey is a research-recruitment and information platform. It is not a research ethics committee, healthcare provider or guarantor of individual research outcomes.

Although studies listed on the platform undergo a verification process, participants should independently satisfy themselves about the authenticity of the study, the researcher and the institution before participating. Verification of a study by GujPsychSurvey is not a guarantee of the safety or authenticity of every external survey link.

Participation is entirely voluntary. You may decline any study or discontinue participation in accordance with the study's consent and withdrawal arrangements.

GujPsychSurvey does not guarantee that participation will provide direct personal benefit, medical advice or treatment.

5. Why your participation matters: mental-health research in Gujarat

Mental-health research needs the participation of people from the communities it intends to understand and serve. Research conducted in other countries or regions may not fully reflect the experiences, languages, cultural practices, social circumstances and healthcare needs of people living in Gujarat.

Participation can help researchers:

  • Understand the mental-health needs and experiences of people across Gujarat.
  • Develop and evaluate Gujarati-language psychological assessment tools.
  • Study the influence of family, education, relationships, employment and social circumstances on mental health.
  • Understand awareness, attitudes, stigma and help-seeking behaviour.
  • Identify gaps in access to mental-health services.
  • Develop culturally appropriate preventive and therapeutic interventions.
  • Generate evidence that can inform mental-health services and public-health policies.

People from different age groups, educational backgrounds, occupations, socioeconomic circumstances and geographical locations all have valuable experiences to contribute.

Research needs more than patients and clinical samples. It also needs the voices of healthy individuals, students, families and communities.

Your participation in a research study is a voluntary contribution to building better scientific knowledge and improving mental-health care in Gujarat.
